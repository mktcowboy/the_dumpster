3 · How to actually buy wholesale

You don’t buy off an exchange. You buy through a jobber / distributor — and the contract structure matters more than the brand on the truck.

RouteTypical sizeBest forTrade-off
Fuel card / retailPer fillSmall or scattered use, road trucks away from baseEasiest, worst price
Tank wagon20 – 4,400 gal / dropDay tanks, jobsite tanks, generators — both streamsFlexible, higher freight per gallon
Transport load7,500+ galBulk storage with steady burnLowest ¢/gal, needs storage + volume
Wet-hose / mobile fuelingPer vehicle / machineFleets fueled overnight in your yard, logged per unitSaves driver time, adds a service fee

The standard contract: “rack plus”

Example: OPIS + 3¢ means if OPIS reports $3.10 at your rack that day, you pay $3.13 wholesale — before freight and taxes. Your four negotiation levers:

  1. Which rack you’re pinned to — there are usually 2–4 nearby, and that choice alone can swing 1–4¢/gal.
  2. The “plus” — the jobber’s differential / margin.
  3. Freight — flat vs per-gallon, minimum drop, split-load and after-hours fees (critical for mixed: a split clear/red drop may be priced differently).
  4. Terms — prompt-pay discount, credit, volume commitment.
Why 1 cent matters: 1¢/gal on 30,000 gal/month = $3,600/year. On 100,000 gal/month = $12,000/year. Get 2–3 quotes, force each to break out rack, differential, freight, taxes, and fees separately, and audit invoices against OPIS / DTN for that day.

4 · Red vs clear — same fuel, different tax

Both are ultra-low sulfur diesel to ASTM D975, max 15 ppm sulfur. Dyed diesel only adds red dye (Solvent Red 164) to mark it as sold without federal highway tax. The dye does not change performance. The tax status changes everything else.

Clear — On-roadRed / Dyed — Off-road
Use inLicensed trucks & any vehicle on public roadsTractors, combines, construction equipment that never goes on public roads, generators, irrigation pumps, reefer units with their own engine & tank
Federal tax24.4¢/gal (24.3¢ highway + 0.1¢ LUST)Only the 0.1¢ LUST tax for nontaxable uses
State taxOklahoma diesel ~19–20¢/gal; US state average 35.5¢ (Jan 2026)Not applied to qualifying off-road use
Your OK saving—Roughly 43–44¢/gal vs clear, before other fees. National combined gap ranges $0.2430 (RI) to $0.9350 (PA)
Never—A licensed / farm truck on public roads, even for farm business — outside the temporary relief window discussed below

The penalty math — why segregation is non-negotiable

  • Federal penalty: greater of $1,000 or $10 per gallon involved, plus back tax — and the $1,000 floor climbs $1,000 per repeat violation.
  • Example: 150 gal of dyed fuel found in a highway tank = $1,500 penalty + $36.60 back excise tax, before any state penalty.
  • Dye lingers in tanks, lines, and filters. One tank of red does not wash out cleanly — a trace can still read as a violation. Never “borrow” from the red tank for a road truck.
  • If you buy taxed clear diesel for a nontaxable use by mistake, you can usually claim it back on IRS Form 4136 or 8849 — the reverse direction has no such fix.
Fall 2026 temporary relief — read carefully: Federal relief covers highway use Oct 1 – Dec 31, 2026: penalties paused and federal tax deferred, not forgiven. State taxes and state rules still apply. In Oklahoma, the governor asked agencies to ease enforcement for farmers/ranchers using dyed diesel on-road for ~120 days around harvest — that is an enforcement-priority request, not a clean legal pass. Do not build your operation on this window. If you use it at all, get it in writing from your CPA first, track every gallon, set aside the deferred tax, and have a flush-and-filter plan before Jan 1, 2027.

5 · Running a mixed operation — the operating plan

This is where mixed businesses win or lose. The fuel is identical; your system has to keep the two tax streams provably separate.

ControlOn-road (clear)Off-road (red)
StorageDedicated clear tank, labeled “CLEAR DIESEL — ON-ROAD”Separate dyed tank, labeled “DYED DIESEL — NONTAXABLE USE ONLY, PENALTY FOR TAXABLE USE”
Nozzles / keysStandard fill for road fleetDifferent nozzle, lock, or keyed access — make the wrong fill physically awkward, not just against policy
VehiclesEvery licensed / road-registered unit, no exceptionsOnly equipment that never enters a public road; list each unit by ID
LoggingGallons per vehicle, odometer, dateGallons per machine, hour-meter, job / field, date
DeliveriesSeparate delivery ticket, clear product codeSeparate delivery ticket, dyed product code — never accept a blended / unspecified drop
PeopleTrain every driver/operator: red never goes in a road tank — not “just to get back to the yard,” not during the relief window without a written policy. One mistake contaminates the tank.

Audit-ready checklist

  • ☐ Two tanks, two labels, two product codes, two invoice lines — on every single delivery
  • ☐ Written list of eligible off-road units (ID / serial) posted at the red tank
  • ☐ Monthly reconciliation: red gallons delivered vs red gallons logged to eligible units
  • ☐ Keep delivery tickets + use logs at least 3 years
  • ☐ No shared transfer tanks / nurse tanks between streams unless dedicated and labeled
  • ☐ If a road vehicle is ever filled from the wrong tank: stop, document, do not drive it on-road, call your CPA / supplier before moving it
  • ☐ Calendar reminder: relief window ends Dec 31, 2026 — normal rules Jan 1

6 · Futures & hedging — insurance, not betting

Hedging locks in a known cost so a spike doesn’t eat your margin. If prices fall, you miss the savings. That trade-off is the product.

The biggest trap: basis risk. Your hedge tracks New York Harbor. Your equipment fills in Oklahoma. A Gulf hurricane, pipeline outage, or regional refinery problem can move your Group 3 rack while ULSD futures barely react. That’s why the strongest programs layer physical contracts (no basis risk — covers both your clear and red streams at the rack) under any financial hedge (basis risk — size it only against the volume you can’t fix physically).

A laddered hedge — never 100%

A common guideline for financial hedging, refreshed monthly and net of any surcharge pass-through you already have. Never hedge 100% of forecast use — if your volume drops, you’re over-hedged.

QuarterHedge share of forecastBar
Nearest quarter40–55%
Second quarter25–35%
Third quarter10–20%
Fourth quarter5–10%

Reality check for scale: for most businesses under ~50 trucks, exchange futures are usually impractical. The practical hedge stack is: negotiated fuel-card discounts for road units away from base + bulk contracts with 30–90 day fixed pricing for both tanks + a well-structured surcharge where you bill customers.

7 · What the forward curve is telling you

The market is in steep backwardation — near-term delivery costs far more than later delivery. NY Harbor futures were $5.26/gal for October delivery on Sep 15 vs $3.59 for September 2027; recent listings still slope down (Nov ~$4.10, Dec ~$3.94, mid-2027 ~$3.25–3.35).

  • Don’t lock 12 months at today’s pump price. Long-dated fixed quotes should be anchored to the forward curve, not spot.
  • Don’t overstock in backwardation — holding inventory is costly when future prices are lower. This applies to both tanks.
  • Layer instead: lock more of the near quarter, less further out, re-price monthly.
  • Push back on a jobber who quotes a 12-month fixed price near today’s spot — ask him to show you the curve he priced it off.

8 · Winter prep — do it now

  • Standard #2 diesel clouds around 6–14°F depending on source; gelling follows.
  • Blending in #1 diesel lowers cloud point about 3°F per 10% blend.
  • Plan the switch when overnight temps approach freezing — for both streams, and confirm your jobber’s winter blend / additive policy for dyed and clear separately.
  • Generators on red diesel are the classic winter failure: test-run them before the first freeze, not during an outage.
