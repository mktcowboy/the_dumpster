# Microgreens — Commercial Rack Operation

Companion to the room schematic: `microgreens-room-schematic.html` (10'×12' floor plan, rack cross-section, 10-day cycle diagram).

Strategy: **organic certification + nice restaurants**, living trays, filtered strictly for **big ticket & high margin**.

---

## 1. Rack setup (per rack)

Standard commercial unit: 48"W × 24"D × 72"H NSF wire shelving on casters, 5 tiers. One shelf holds four 10×20 trays → ~20 trays in production per rack.

| Item | Spec | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Wire rack, 5-tier, casters | Seville Classics 48×24×72 | ~$229 |
| LED bars | Barrina 20W T5 6500K, 12-pack (3 per shelf) | $80–90 |
| Fans | AC Infinity 120mm, 3× 2-pack (1 per shelf) | ~$81 |
| Timer + power | Digital timer, outlet multiplier, ext. cord | ~$40–50 |
| Trays | Bootstrap Farmer 1020 extra-strength (grow + solid bottom-water trays) | $6.45–7.65 ea |
| Media | Coco coir | ~$1/tray |

**One rack all-in: ~$500–600** before trays/seed. Key practices: bottom-water only (solid tray under holed tray, dump excess), RH under 60%, 14–16h light, weighted blackout germination 2–4 days, sanitize trays between cycles with food-grade H₂O₂.

## 2. Room schematic

10'×12' room, 3 racks (60 tray slots), work table (seed/harvest/pack), sink, fridge at 38°F, dehumidifier + AC (non-negotiable in Oklahoma summers). Bottom tier of each rack = blackout germination; upper 4 tiers = growing. Stagger sowings every 2–3 days → harvest twice weekly. **~30+ trays/week capacity.**

## 3. Unit economics (per 1020 tray)

| Crop | Seed cost | Yield | Cycle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Radish | ~$2.00 | 8–11 oz | 7–10 days |
| Broccoli | ~$0.30–0.50 | 10–12 oz | 8–12 days |
| Sunflower | ~$1–2 | 16–24 oz | 9–10 days |
| Pea shoots | ~$1–2 | 12–24 oz | 9–12 days |

Full cost stack per tray: seed $0.35–2 + media ~$1 + power ~$0.25 + labor ~$5 + packaging (retail) $1–3 → **~$8–11 all-in**. Wholesale living tray $25–40; retail equivalent $40+/tray in small clamshells. Example margins: Missouri extension budget mix tray — $46 revenue, $30.06 cost, **$15.94 margin**; sunflower wholesale tray at $15 → **$5.80 net**.

Startup: lean 1-rack $500–600; small-commercial 10-rack $7,500–12,000 + fridge. **No rent** until revenue justifies it — $500/mo rent takes a one-shelf operation to ~95% capacity just to break even.

## 4. Where the real $ is

Margin ladder (best → worst): (1) small retail packs direct/independent grocers — 2oz clamshell at $5–7 = $2.50–3.50/oz, **5.4× the per-oz rate of wholesale**; (2) subscription/standing weekly orders; (3) farmers markets; (4) restaurants (~10–15% of revenue for profitable growers — credibility + consistency, not the foundation); (5) wholesale distributors (commodity, margins die).

## 5. Organic certification path

- **Stage 1 (immediate):** <$5,000/yr exemption — can market as "organic" following all NOP rules (organic seed, approved media like coir, no synthetics, keep records), but no USDA seal.
- **Stage 2:** full certification via USDA-accredited certifier. Small-farm cost typically $1,000–1,500/yr; USDA cost-share reimburses 75% up to $750. Timeline 3–6 months. No 3-year land transition for indoor container growing (fresh media).
- Buy organic seed from tray one; keep every receipt — records are needed for certification anyway.

## 6. Restaurant playbook

Product = **living trays** delivered alive, cut to order ($25–35 organic). Pitch Tue–Thu 2–4pm (never during service) with a free sample tray + one-page variety/price sheet. Requirements: product liability insurance (~$560/yr, $1M per occurrence — get before first pitch), written food safety plan, invoices/lot traceability, cold delivery, same delivery day every week. Reliability beats variety; expect 10–20% annual account churn (chef turnover) — keep sampling forever.

Oklahoma: no license needed to sell own-grown fresh produce at farmers markets; >$25k/yr produce sales → register (free) with ODAFF Produce Safety Program.

## 7. Highest margin × highest use varieties

| Variety | Margin/day per tray-slot | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Radish (Rambo/China Rose) | ~$3.30 | Fastest turn (8d); chefs ask by name |
| Broccoli | ~$2.80 | Seed nearly free; sulforaphane story |
| Pea shoots/tendrils | ~$2.45 | Chef's darling; massive yield |
| Sunflower | ~$2.45 | Cheapest seed, heaviest yield; volume workhorse |

Premium tier (highest $/tray, lower volume): shiso, Thai/lemon/opal basil, nasturtium, amaranth, sorrel — $35–45+/tray living. Use as differentiators, not the base. Slot allocation: ~60% core four, ~25% account requests, ~15% experimenting.

## 8. Mushrooms — big-ticket filtered

Same buyer, same truck, higher ticket. **One hard rule:** separate space — mushrooms fruit at 80–95% RH, greens die above 60%. Use a 4×4/4×8 grow tent ($100–200) as fruiting chamber.

**Keep:** Lion's mane, own blocks (~$3), organic, direct to chefs — $8–12/lb wholesale ($16+ organic-leaning), ~$12–17 margin per block. The fine-dining mushroom (crab texture, medicinal story).
**Kill:** blue/grey oyster wholesale ($6–8/lb, commodity); bought fruiting blocks as a business model (margin stays with maker — buy once to test demand only); indoor shiitake (more work, less margin); dried/powdered (different business).

Real big ticket = account size: one chef at 6 living trays ($180) + 5 lb lion's mane ($60)/week ≈ $12.5k/yr per account. Sell a weekly "chef's box" bundle, not à la carte.

**Sequencing:** months 1–3 microgreens cash-flowing → month 3–4 tent with bought blocks to test chef demand → invest in sterilizer/lab only on proven pull.

## 9. Definitions

All listed varieties are microgreens. Pea/sunflower sometimes labeled "shoots" (harvested taller) — same category commercially. **Sprouts ≠ microgreens:** jar-grown sprouts carry stricter food-safety scrutiny and outbreak history — don't grow them.
