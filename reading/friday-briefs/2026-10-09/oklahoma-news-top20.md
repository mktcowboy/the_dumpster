**1. Red River Rivalry Saturday: OU faces No. 1 Texas in Dallas** (statewide / OU athletics)

**2. Cheyenne and Arapaho Tribes approve 3-year data-center moratorium** (statewide / tribal)
Date: voted Sat Oct 3, reported Mon Oct 5. At their annual Tribal Council meeting in Concho, citizens voted 261–83 to halt data-center construction on tribal lands through October 2029. Concerns cited included water use, environmental impacts, and transparency; tribal Gov. Reggie Wassana said he still supports development and no deal is signed. Follows other Oklahoma tribes adopting similar pauses.
Source: [News 9](https://www.news9.com/oklahoma-city-news/oklahoma-cheyenne-arapaho-data-center-moratorium)

**3. OTA pays $20.5M for Norman's HeyDay Entertainment property** (Cleveland County)
Date: reported Tue Oct 7. Records obtained by News 9 show the Oklahoma Turnpike Authority paid $20,533,040 for the HeyDay Entertainment property in Norman as part of the Toby Keith Expressway's 28-mile corridor expansion — over 8x the property's county valuation of ~$2.5M. OTA says it initially needed only 0.09 acres and HeyDay asked the authority to buy the whole property; the indoor-playground/mini-golf business has closed.
Source: [News 9](https://www.news9.com/oklahoma-city-news/5-things-to-know-ota-purchases-norman-heyday-sayre-grain-fire)

**4. New state superintendent sworn in; Walters-era Bible mandate repealed** (statewide / education)
Date: Thu Oct 8. Lindel Fields was sworn in as state superintendent and said his priority is lifting Oklahoma's literacy rates, launching a 90-day turnaround plan and an internal audit of his predecessor's actions. The Bible mandate — Ryan Walters' order requiring a Bible in every classroom — was repealed, and the Oklahoma Supreme Court had already blocked the purchases. Walters resigned Sept. 24 and is now CEO of the Teacher Freedom Alliance.
Sources: [The Collegian (TU)](https://tucollegian.org/15608-2/); [The Collegian — Walters resignation](https://tucollegian.org/oklahoma-state-superintendent-ryan-walters-officially-resigns/)

**5. Armed robbery at Cleveland County cannabis grow near Lexington** (Cleveland County)
Date: crime Oct 2; sheriff's press conference Fri Oct 9. Sheriff Darrell Weaver said masked, heavily armed robbers cased the Kai Family Farm grow near Lexington, then attacked — beating workers with firearms, including one victim struck with what deputies believe was the butt of an AK-47, and taking phones and keys to block calls for help. Weaver framed the "grow rip" as emblematic of rising violence targeting the state's marijuana industry.
Source: [The Oklahoman](https://www.oklahoman.com/story/news/crime/2026/10/09/cleveland-county-sheriff-oklahoma-marijuana-grow-robbery/92176493007/)

**6. ODOT executive director Tim Gatz retiring; TJ Dill named interim** (statewide / infrastructure)
Date: Thu Oct 8. Gov. Stitt named ODOT chief engineer TJ Dill interim director after Tim Gatz announced his retirement effective Oct. 31. Gatz, who has served as secretary of transportation since 2019, will keep that cabinet role through the end of Stitt's term. Dill, an El Reno native living in Yukon, has been at ODOT since 2013.
Source: [News 9](https://www.news9.com/oklahoma-city-news/interim-director-of-oklahoma-department-of-transportation)

**7. Oklahoma Emergency Management director Annie Mack Vest leaving Oct. 30** (statewide)
Date: announced Tue Oct 6. Vest, who has led the agency since December 2023, is leaving for the private sector; Chief Operations Officer John Dean (state EM since 2022, formerly 20 years Army Special Forces) takes over as interim. Stitt credited her with steering Oklahoma through multiple tornado seasons and unprecedented wildfire events.
Source: [News 9](https://www.news9.com/oklahoma-city-news/5-things-to-know-dill-city-standoff-arrest-okc-metro-highway-closures)

**8. One dead in Duncan officer-involved shooting** (statewide / crime)
Date: Fri Oct 9. Duncan police stopped a vehicle around 2:43 a.m. near 5th Street and Beech Avenue, connected to an unauthorized-use incident with the driver believed armed. OSBI is conducting the independent investigation and officers involved are on administrative leave; identity not yet released.

**17. Moore Animal Welfare flags first confirmed case of Bobcat Fever** (Cleveland County / health)
Date: Wed Oct 7. Moore's Animal Welfare and Adoption Center reported its first confirmed case of Bobcat Fever (Cytauxzoonosis), a tick-borne parasite disease of domestic cats that progresses rapidly and is often fatal even with treatment. The center is urging pet owners to use tick prevention; no bobcat encounter is required for transmission.
Source: [News 9](https://www.news9.com/oklahoma-city-news/5-things-to-know-ota-purchases-norman-heyday-sayre-grain-fire)
