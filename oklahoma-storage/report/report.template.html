Statewide supply, company portfolios, capital relationships, rents and property offerings.

Research edition · October 19, 2026

A statewide market with distinct local economies

The directory is a useful starting point for understanding who operates where. The strongest conclusions concern market structure; revenue, occupancy and total rentable supply still require deeper property-level evidence.

What the evidence says

  1. Look beyond the two major cities. The baseline places 473 of 865 records outside its OKC and Tulsa groupings. Those 54.7% of records make secondary markets a substantial part of the research universe. Directory groupings are not official metro boundaries.
  2. Consolidation changes the company map. Legacy SecurCare and Public Storage brands now sit under the Public Storage platform following the NSA closing in July 2026. Life Storage belongs with Extra Space.
  3. Capital relationships differ from property ownership. Prime and Morningstar disclose PE structures; SpareBox discloses a fund-backed joint venture. U-Haul is a public operating company. Storelocal allows separate owners to share a brand.
  4. Pricing needs a consistent denominator. Web promotions, posted unit prices, mixed-amenity regional averages, parking and in-place rents are different measures. This report keeps them separate.

National backdrop

Yardi’s September 2026 update reported national advertised storage rates down 1.9% year over year in August, following declines of 1.6% in July and 1.5% in June. It links weak pricing to soft demand and recent supply. This is national context, not an Oklahoma rent-growth estimate; the report does not extrapolate it into a local forecast.

Rental pricing: evidence, not an invented market average

Prices were reviewed October 19, 2026 through publicly available pages and search-index snapshots. Indexed observations may predate review. No reservations or checkout purchases were made, and fees were not fully captured.

One operator’s regional advertised averages

Extra Space near-city pages mix amenities and surrounding locations. These are operator-published figures, not an independent or statewide market survey. The opened page snapshot takes precedence over conflicting search snippets.

In this snapshot, Extra Space’s published 10×10 average is $54 near Oklahoma City and $88 near Tulsa. The difference is not a same-store or same-product premium: the underlying facilities and amenity mix differ.

Facility-level price observations

Read the billing basis. Durant’s site publishes dollar amounts without explicitly stating a billing period; those observations are excluded from monthly comparisons. Parking is never treated as enclosed storage. Promotions are recorded separately rather than applied a second time to web prices.

Coverage is selective. These observations cover a small set of facilities and cities. Blank company pricing means no comparable quote was captured, not that units are unavailable or free.

How to make the next pricing survey comparable

  1. Sample 5×5, 5×10, 10×10, 10×15 and 10×20 units separately for climate-controlled and non-climate-controlled space. Record floor, elevator access, drive-up access and availability.
  2. Save standard rent, online rent, promotion duration, mandatory recurring protection/insurance, one-time admin and lock charges, taxes, and the stated rent-change policy.
  3. Calculate quoted first-year cost only when all inputs and their periods are known. An initial quote is not a 12-month rate guarantee.
  4. For market trends, revisit the same facilities and product types on a consistent schedule. Report sample size and missing quotes alongside any median.

Property offerings & closed transactions

Broker-published offerings are separated from stale or unverified marketplace leads and announced closed sales. A public page establishes marketing evidence, not confirmed availability. Asking prices and advertised cap rates are not sale comps.

What to verify before comparing deals

Reconcile rentable area with unit mix, especially parking and retail. Separate physical occupancy from economic occupancy. Obtain current rent rolls, trailing operating statements, tax and insurance quotes, delinquency reports, management contracts, deferred maintenance and parcel records. Underwriting changes materially when advertised cap rates use stabilized rather than in-place income.

Development and expansion claims require an independent check of zoning, approvals, site work, drainage, utilities and the construction budget. This edition does not claim to inventory all projects under construction. The closed Stillwater transaction has no disclosed consideration and therefore cannot establish a price-per-square-foot benchmark.

Coverage audit

How company profiles are assembled

  1. Normalize explicit aliases. Extra Space / Life Storage, Public Storage / legacy SecurCare, Arborstone variants, SpareBox variants, Storelocal capitalization and Storage R Us variants are grouped. Current parent relationships carry external citations.
  2. Use shared operator domains as association evidence. Reviewed business domains connect differently named locations. Social sites, marketplaces and generic software platforms are not used as proof of common ownership.
  3. Keep uncertainty visible. A directory brand, shared website, third-party manager, affiliate network and deed owner are distinct concepts. Unknown capital backing stays unknown. A company profile never turns a directory assignment into a verified deed record.
  4. Preserve possible duplicates. Matching normalized street addresses trigger a review flag; records are not silently deleted. Generic equal names without additional evidence are not automatically consolidated across towns. Multiple records may represent one physical site.
  5. Link research conservatively. Prices and offerings match on normalized address or exact name within the city. Matches are candidates for property reconciliation, particularly where rebrands share an address. Research-only offerings get separate profiles and do not inflate baseline facility totals.

Known limitations

The original README states that place searches and directory cross-checks were performed on October 19, 2026. Per-record original search citations and collection timestamps are not available. Published websites in the baseline are useful leads but have not all been revisited. Coverage is neither exhaustive nor a verified count of distinct operating facilities.

The 558 raw “independent” labels are source classifications, not verified ownership conclusions. Conversely, U-Haul affiliation does not prove company ownership; Storelocal branding explicitly permits separate property owners. Smaller operators may have capital partners not disclosed publicly. Historical sponsor disclosures retain their original dates.

No market-wide unit count, rentable area, rent roll, revenue or occupancy dataset is available. This report deliberately leaves those measures unestimated. The 127 city-field labels include county labels and are not a set of standardized trade areas; approximate geocoding cannot support reliable radius-based supply calculations.

Address conflicts requiring review

Same normalized numeric street address within a city; not an automatic duplicate verdict. Shared facilities, rebrands and source errors are all possible.
