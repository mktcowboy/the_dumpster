# Cryptography — IACR ePrint Top 20, Sep 18 – Oct 18, 2026 (scout report, received Oct 18, 2026)

## On statistics
IACR used to publish "Top 20 Downloads" reports — in the IACR Newsletter (Vol. 25 No. 2, Vol. 26 No. 1 and No. 2, covering 2010–2011; e.g. 2004/314 with 22,474 downloads). Those are the last ones that exist. There is no current public download/view ranking anywhere — not on the ePrint site, not in recent newsletters. The archive's stats page covers submission counts per year only. So the list below is curated by significance, not usage — IACR publishes no download rankings to rank from.

## The list
1. [Forging 1024-bit RSA signatures in nearly SNFS time](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2131) — Laura Shea, Miro Haller, Adam Suhl, Nadia Heninger, Emmanuel Thomé (2026/2131, Sep 20) — Adapts the special number field sieve to forge RSA signatures from a blind-signing oracle at ~2^65 work instead of ~2^80 factoring; got wide press; hits raw/blind RSA (Privacy Pass-style), not padded RSA.
2. [Every Signing Leaks: Breaking Falcon via Floating-Point Conversion Leakage](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2124) — Yuanyuan Zhou, Weijia Wang, Yiteng Sun, Yu Yu (2026/2124, ~Sep 19) — Full key recovery against Falcon, NIST's selected post-quantum signature scheme, via floating-point conversion side channels.
3. [Breaking First-Order Masked Kyber with Belief Propagation Alone](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2448) — Vahid Jahandideh, Lejla Batina (2026/2448, ~Oct 10) — Defeats first-order-masked ML-KEM (Kyber, FIPS 203) implementations with pure belief propagation; says current masking isn't enough for the deployed standard.
4. [Too Small to Hide: Single-Trace Key Recovery from ML-KEM Key Generation](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2137) — Vahid Jahandideh (2026/2137, ~Sep 20) — One power/EM trace of key generation suffices to steal an ML-KEM private key on vulnerable embedded targets.
5. [Superposition Key-Recovery Attacks on Dilithium and Fiat-Shamir Signature Schemes](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2258) — Carlos Cid, David Elkouss, Manuel Goulão (2026/2258, ~Sep 28) — Quantum attackers with superposition query access can recover ML-DSA/Dilithium keys; new quantum cryptanalysis of the standardized PQ signature.
6. [Recovering SNOVA Secret Keys from Biased Vinegar Sampling](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2154) — Ward Beullens, Basil Hess (2026/2154, ~Sep 21) — Secret-key recovery against SNOVA, a NIST additional-signatures candidate, from a leading multivariate cryptanalyst.
7. [Geometric Forgeries: Structural Cryptanalysis of MAYO](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2247) — Massimo Ostuzzi (2026/2247, ~Sep 27) — Structural forgery attack on MAYO, a live NIST additional-signatures round-2 candidate.
8. [Reverse-Bleichenbacher: Bleichenbacher Attacks are Practical Again](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2414) — Youheng Lü, Lorenz Hetterich, Riccardo Zanotto, Cas Cremers, Michael Schwarz, Lukas Gerlach (2026/2414, ~Oct 8) — Flips the Bleichenbacher padding oracle around, making RSA PKCS#1 v1.5 attacks practical again against real deployments.
9. [Argo: Leaking a Secret from an Invalid Groth16 Proof](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2165) — Liam Eagen, Ying Tong Lai (2026/2165, ~Sep 22) — An invalid Groth16 proof can leak the prover's secret; alarming because Groth16 is the most widely deployed SNARK in production.
10. [MultLA: A Framework of Fast Correlation Attack Exploiting Multiple Linear Approximations and Its Application to SNOW 5G](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2197) — Jiang Wan, Lin Ding, Jiekun Sun, Xinhai Wang (2026/2197, ~Sep 24) — New fast-correlation-attack framework using multiple linear approximations, applied to SNOW 5G, the cipher in 5G networks.
11. [MiniSHAKE](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2363) — Guido Bertoni, Joan Daemen, Seth Hoffert, Silvia Mella, Michaël Peeters, Gilles Van Assche, Ronny Van Keer (2026/2363, ~Oct 4) — Compact new SHAKE variant from the original Keccak/SHA-3 design team.
12. [MIKE: a fast and compact post-quantum NIKE](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2377) — Andrea Basso, Pierrick Dartois, Max Duparc, Jonathan Komada Eriksen, Sabrina Kunzweiler, Michael Meyer, Giacomo Pope, Krijn Reĳnders, Damien Robert, Ryan Rueger, Sina Schaeffler (2026/2377, ~Oct 5) — Fast, compact post-quantum non-interactive key exchange; NIKE is notoriously hard post-quantum, and the isogeny-heavy author list makes it credible.
13. [Post-Quantum PKE and More from a Noisy Unstructured Linear Algebraic Assumption: Beyond LWE and LPN](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2113) — Riddhi Ghosal, Paul Lou, Amit Sahai (2026/2113, ~Sep 19) — PQ public-key encryption from a new "noisy unstructured linear algebra" assumption — a fresh hardness foundation that sidesteps LWE/LPN structure.
14. [It Takes Two: Proofs of Work for Fiat–Shamir](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2326) — Benedikt Bünz, Jessica Chen, Ziyi Guan (2026/2326, ~Oct 2) — Hardens the Fiat-Shamir transform (used by virtually all deployed SNARKs) by requiring proofs of work alongside it, blunting proof-grinding attacks.
15. [Accordion: Efficient Batch Proving via Algebraic Folding](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2160) — David Balbás, Anca Nitulescu, Maxime Plançon (2026/2160, ~Sep 22) — Cheaper batch proving for SNARKs via algebraic folding: prove many statements at once for much less.
16. [SAK: Sparse Arguments of Knowledge, from Sparse Lookup Arguments](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2143) — Abhiram Kothapalli, Sriram Sridhar, Arantxa Zapico (2026/2143, ~Sep 21) — Succinct arguments of knowledge tailored to sparse computations, built from sparse lookups.
17. [Zippel: A Language, Compiler, Runtime, and Analyzer for ZK Proof Systems](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2517) — Sebastian Angel, Sydnie-Shea Cohen, Eleftherios Ioannidis, Pratyush Mishra, Alex Ozdemir, Yuxiang Qiu, Alireza Shirzad (2026/2517, ~Oct 14) — Full-stack ZK toolchain (language + compiler + runtime + analyzer) from heavyweight systems authors; infrastructure that could reshape how proofs are built and audited.
18. [CKKS Bootstrapping in less than 10ms](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2119) — Rending Ouyang, Jian Liu, Peimin Gao, Pan Xiao, Heng Zhang, Man Ho Au, Cong Zhang, Kui Ren, Chun Chen (2026/2119, ~Sep 19) — Sub-10ms CKKS bootstrapping, a practical milestone for the FHE scheme behind encrypted ML inference.
19. [Levis: Extension-Free Multi-Key Fully Homomorphic Encryption in the Plain Model](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2263) — Baoyu Li, Binwu Xiang, Kang Yang (2026/2263, ~Sep 28) — Multi-key FHE with no extension overhead in the plain model — removes the main cost of computing on ciphertexts encrypted under different keys.
20. [Breaking the $n^2$ Barrier: Information-Theoretic MPC with Sub-Quadratic Communication](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2311) — Alexander Bienstock, Yuval Efron, Kevin Yeo (2026/2311, ~Oct 1) — First information-theoretic MPC with sub-quadratic communication; breaks the long-standing n² barrier.

Honorable mentions: 2460 cube attacks on Trivium; 2490 reaction attack on CKKS deployments; 2501 Aerie (Falcon signature aggregation); 2141 BitZ (proofs/commitments via binary fields); 2157 Jolt-QED (formal verification of a zkVM in Lean); 2099 succinct arguments for QMA; 2063 witness encryption from SNARGs; 2426 claimed subexponential LWE algorithm — treat with heavy scrutiny; 2388 faster provable lattice sieving.

## Methodology
Window = papers posted Sep 18 – Oct 18, 2026 ≈ ePrint 2026/2060–2522 (numbers sequential; anchored at 2026/2131 posted Sep 20, 2026, ~16 papers/day). Three workers each read every listing entry in a third of that range (title + authors, via the eprint-classic mirror), nominated their 8 strongest, merged 24 ranked down to 20. Ranking criteria, in order: (1) breaks/attacks on deployed systems or standards-track PQC; (2) new primitives or models; (3) major concrete efficiency gains; (4) notable author teams / visible community pickup; (5) subfield diversity (ZK, FHE, MPC, PQC, symmetric, signatures, cryptanalysis). "Top" = editorial judgment of novelty + potential impact.

## Caveats
ePrint is unrefereed — attack claims are as-claimed by authors, not verified results. Posting dates marked "~" estimated from sequential numbering (exact only for 2026/2131). Text-fetch proxy was blocked from loading eprint.iacr.org paper pages directly (403s), so metadata verified via mirror listing, indexed copies, and secondary coverage (crypto press, ZK community roundups) — URLs are the canonical eprint.iacr.org/2026/NNNN form corroborated across sources. Adjacent context, not an ePrint paper: Samuel Neves factored RSA-896 on Sep 19, 2026 using Claude-orchestrated GPU sieving (~30 GPU-years over 10 days) — written up on his blog, not posted to ePrint.
