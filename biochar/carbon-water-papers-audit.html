Audit: Science Papers on Carbon-Based Water Filtration

Is the work repository's biochar/research collection the best it can be for citing biochar and activated-carbon water treatment?

Prepared November 5, 2026 (America/Chicago) · Read-only GitHub inventory + Crossref/OpenAlex verification

Verdict
  • No — not for water filtration citing. The Science/ and Sources/ hubs are strong for permanence, CDR and soil, but they are not a curated library of peer-reviewed water-treatment papers.
  • For carbon-based contaminant removal, usable assets are thin: two Water-purification HTML notes, a vendor Norit pack, Production notes (PFAS.md, Biochar vs Activated Carbon.md), and a “top 20” list that is mostly soil/climate or off-topic for filtration.
  • No peer-reviewed paper PDFs were found under biochar/research/ (GitHub code search for extension:pdf returned 0). The only PDFs are Norit marketing/case-study PDFs.
  • There is no master BibTeX / CSL-JSON / CSV bibliography for water. Citation metadata is scattered across Markdown/HTML notes.
  • Your standalone report at /workspace/biochar-activated-carbon-water-report.html already cites a stronger water set (25 sources) than the Science folder; most of those DOIs are not curated as first-class entries in the repo.

3. Problems found

  1. Wrong mental model of the folder. Science/ = permanence/CDR knowledge base, not a filtration paper database.
  2. No full texts of the studies you need to cite (paywall/access limits noted in summaries.md; Elsevier denied; most entries abstract-only).
  3. Wrong or stale DOIs still live in the editable top-20 list.
  4. Over-weight on vendor evidence (Norit) relative to peer-reviewed primary studies for performance claims.
  5. Duplicate MD/HTML without a single canonical bibliography row (title, authors, year, journal, DOI, topic tags, tier, PDF path).
  6. Missing standards corpus for drinking water: NSF/ANSI 42/53/61, AWWA B604 (GAC), EPA PFAS BAT documents are cited in notes/report but not filed under Standards/.
  7. Weak recent (2022–2026) water coverage as curated paper entries, despite useful Production/PFAS narrative notes.
  8. Summaries honesty is good (basis tags, corrections)—but the source list was left uncorrected, so a hurried citer can still grab a wrong DOI.

5. Gap list

  • Landmark water reviews missing as first-class entries: Li 2017 metals; Rajapaksha 2016 engineered biochar; Ambaye 2020 capacities; Qiu 2022; Zhang 2020 nutrients; Heidarinejad 2020 AC activation; Cantoni 2021 GAC–PFAS; Peiris 2017 antibiotics.
  • Recent PFAS work (2022–2026): Liang et al. 2024 critical review; Wang 2023 thermal enhancement; Mortazavian 2025 RSSCT GAC (in water report, not curated); short-chain PFAS engineered biochars.
  • Pathogens / household filters: only lightly covered (Kranner 2019 in report; CDC/NSF in certification note). No systematic literature folder.
  • Taste/odor: Cook 2001 PAC MIB/geosmin in report only.
  • Stormwater biofilters: Chen 2022 review in report; not curated in Science.
  • Meta-analyses and column/field validation beyond Huggins/Thompson/Alhashimi.
  • Master bibliography + PDF vault for any paper you actually quote.

6. Coverage vs biochar-activated-carbon-water-report.html

The October 22, 2026 water report lists 25 references. Of the DOI-bearing ones, only Ahmad 2014 and Alhashimi 2017 (plus bridge papers appearing in the top-20 list) are clearly curated in the research “paper list.” Most report refs (Wang PeerJ, Qiu, Heidarinejad, Thomas, Li, Peiris, Castiglioni, Zhang PFAS, Liu RESCA, Wang 2023, Mortazavian, Zhang nutrients, Chen stormwater, Kranner, Cook, Mayer, …) live in the report and/or Water-purification HTML, not as entries under Science/Sources.

Implication: the report is currently a better cite pack for water than the Science hub. Fold the report’s bibliography into a master water bibliography in the repo rather than treating Science as the authority.

7.5 Recurring literature-alert routine (suggestion)

  • Monthly (30–45 min): OpenAlex/Crossref queries for biochar AND (PFAS OR wastewater OR drinking water OR adsorption) and granular activated carbon AND PFAS, sort by publication date; add anything Tier A/B to the CSV.
  • Quarterly: Refresh OpenAlex citation counts; re-check DOIs; skim EPA/NSF updates on PFAS and drinking-water media.
  • On every new claim: require DOI + tier + summary_basis before the number enters a client report.
