01 / The look

Realism is evidence, not detail.

His feed reads like lost frames from a hip-hop film shot in Rio on expired stock. The realism doesn't come from “more detail.” It comes from grain, bad light, sweat, clutter, and timing — the things a real camera accidentally records.

01

Documentary first

Every frame implies a photographer was standing there. Candid beats composed. The subject is often mid-action, mid-conversation, or leaving the frame.

02

Film is the finish

16mm and 35mm stocks, visible grain, halation, soft corners. Never “8K.” The imperfections are the proof that a lens, not a renderer, made it.

03

Light has a source

Hard afternoon sun, direct on-camera flash, sodium streetlights, blue-hour ambience. Name the source and direction. “Cinematic lighting” alone is a slot machine.

04

Place is a character

Tangled wires, laundry lines, mosaic pavement, heat haze, stacked brick. Three specific clutter details do more than twenty adjectives.

05

Bodies don't pose

Hands in pockets, backs turned, sweat, awkward crops. Eye contact is occasional, not mandatory. Perfection reads as stock photography.

06

One surreal beat

A blimp over the hillside. A gas mask in a stairwell. A giant bird in traffic. Keep the world real and let exactly one thing be wrong.

02 / The formula

Write like a photographer,
not a prompt engineer.

Fill every bracket with something only a real place would have. The chain is: format → subject → place → light → camera → film → flaw → ratio. Break the chain and the AI look creeps back in.

The skeleton 
[shot type] of [very specific subject]
in [hyper-specific real place], [candid action],
[wardrobe + props], [time of day + light source],
shot on [camera + lens], [film stock],
[imperfections], [color palette], [aspect ratio]

04 / Consistency

One world. Many angles.

A campaign isn't 20 images that each look good. It's one world photographed from 20 angles. Lock the film language, then move only the story. The goal is not identical images — it's images that clearly came from the same shoot.

Lock these / the film language

  • Aspect ratio and crop logic
  • Lens set: 24mm, 35mm, 50mm
  • Film stock, grain, and halation level
  • Light logic: hard sun, direct flash, tungsten night
  • Grade: warm highlights, crushed blacks, muted greens
  • Cast, wardrobe palette, and recurring props

Swap one at a time / the story

  • One location detail
  • One action or gesture
  • One framing: wide, medium, close
  • Time of day, only within the same light logic
  • One surreal beat
  • The product or campaign message

Between scenes / the 8-step ritual
01

Finish the hero first

If the hero frame is wrong, every variation inherits the mistake. Don't move on until the grade, grain, and cast read correctly.

02

Save the recipe

Store the exact prompt, seed, model version, reference images, and settings for every keeper. Future you will not remember.

03

Write the shot list first

List all scenes before generating the second one. Otherwise you'll fall in love with a frame that doesn't fit the set.

04

Use the hero as reference

Feed the approved frame back in as an image or style reference. Text alone drifts; a reference anchors the grade and texture.

05

Change one axis per batch

Lens, light, place, or action. Never all four. If you change two, you've made a new look, not a new scene.

06

Build a contact sheet

Judge each scene as a set, not as a single image. Pick for continuity and thumbnail readability.

07

Keep a reject wall

Near-misses tell you which variable is drifting — skin tone, grain, wardrobe, or product shape. Diagnose before re-rolling.

08

Grade at the end

Batch-grade all selects in one pass. Grain and halation go on last, after color and exposure are matched.

Same scene, four ways / the base stays fixed

Film still from a 16mm documentary, two kids playing football on a favela rooftop in Rio de Janeiro at golden hour, laundry lines and tangled wires, 24mm lens, Kodak Vision3 250D, hard sun, visible grain, slight halation, unposed, 4:5

Variation A / gesture

Same frame, one kid looks back over his shoulder, ball mid-air.

Variation B / angle

Same frame, camera two meters lower, looking up past the laundry.

Variation C / lens

Same frame, 50mm, tighter on hands and the ball, rooftop out of focus.

Variation D / light

Same frame, blue hour, streetlights on, same grade and grain.

Notice what did not change: cast, wardrobe, place, stock, grade, ratio. That's why they read as one scene instead of four unrelated images.

05 / Campaign

Run it like a
small film shoot.

Brief, style bible, shot list, selects, grade, legal, test. The model is the camera, not the director. A campaign needs a point of view before it needs more generations.

Phase 00

Brief

One sentence: who, what, where, why. Define audience, platform, product truth, mandatories, and three reference images. If you can't write the concept in one sentence, you're not ready to generate.

Phase 01

Build the style bible

Three anchor frames: a hero portrait, an environment, and a detail. Add the locked spine, the negative prompt, a palette, wardrobe notes, and the lens set. If a frame doesn't match the anchors, it doesn't ship.

Phase 02

Shot list

Six frames maximum for the first pass: establishing, hero, action, detail, crowd, surreal/end card. Give every frame a purpose, a ratio, a prompt, and a reference. No orphan images.

Phase 03

Generate

Four to six variations per frame at the final aspect ratio. Use the same seed and reference for the batch. Keep a contact sheet and a reject wall. Don't crop after the fact if you can generate at the right ratio.

Phase 04

Select

Choose for continuity and thumbnail readability, not for “the best image.” The most impressive frame is often the most AI-looking. Pick the one that feels like it was captured by accident.

Phase 05

Post

Batch-grade every select in one pass. Add grain and halation globally. Use a light unsharp mask, never HDR. Put type in the safe zone and export per platform.

Phase 06

Legal and ethics

Label AI where required. Secure likeness rights. Don't present staged AI favela scenes as documentary. Check product labels and logos. Follow platform ad policy and music licensing.

Phase 07

Test

Three hooks × three visuals × two CTAs. Test the hook first, then the visual. Keep the style spine fixed and vary only the message. Iterate weekly, not daily.

Campaign hero prompt / swap the product, keep the spine

Campaign hero frame for a streetwear launch, 16mm documentary, a young Brazilian skateboarder in an oversized vintage football jersey standing on a favela rooftop at golden hour, scuffed skateboard under one arm, city haze and tangled wires behind, 35mm lens, Kodak Vision3 250D, hard sun, warm highlights, crushed blacks, muted greens, visible grain, slight halation, unposed, 4:5, clean copy space in the upper left

For the next frame, change only the product or the action. Keep the spine, stock, lens, light, and grade identical. That is how a set stays coherent.

Campaign QA / check before export
  • Same grade and grain across every frame
  • Cast and wardrobe read continuously
  • Product label and logo are accurate
  • Hands, reflections, and shadows are sane
  • Copy space exists where type will go
  • No accidental text, watermark, or brand mark
  • No real-person likeness without rights
  • AI disclosure added where required
  • Exports match each platform ratio
  • Thumbnail test: product and face still read
Ad structures that fit this look
A

Carousel: World → Hero → Detail → CTA

Four frames, one story. The world earns attention, the hero carries desire, the detail proves the product, the CTA closes.

B

Video: Hook → World → Product → CTA

0–1s hook, 1–3s world, 3–6s product, 6–8s CTA. Keep the same grade and grain across every cut.

C

Test: 3 hooks × 3 visuals × 2 CTAs

Eighteen combinations is enough for a first read. Don't test style and message at the same time.

D

Iterate: keep the spine, change the hook

If CTR is low, change the first frame. If CPA is high, change the offer. Don't redesign the look to fix a message problem.

The 7-day sprint / one person, one campaign
01

Brief + refs

One sentence, three references, audience, platform, product truth, and the full deliverable list.

02

Style bible + hero

Lock the spine, generate the hero frame, and get approval before making anything else.

03

Shot list + support

Generate the five supporting frames. Four to six variations each, same seed and reference.

04

Selects

Build contact sheets, keep a reject wall, and run a continuity pass across the whole set.

05

Post

Batch grade, add grain and halation, set type in the safe zones, and export every ratio.

06

Legal + exports

Likeness rights, AI disclosure, product accuracy, platform policy, file naming, and handoff.

07

Launch + test

Three hooks, three visuals, two CTAs. Keep the style spine fixed and read the data before making more.

Two people can compress this to three days: one directs and selects, one generates and retouches. The sequence stays the same.

06 / Models

Same prompt,
different camera.

Every generator has a default bias. Midjourney beautifies, Flux sharpens, conversational models clean up clutter. Prompt against the bias, not with it.

07 / Rules

What sells the realism.

A checklist you can run before every export. If a frame fails three or more items, regenerate instead of retouching.

Do

  • Name a real camera, lens, and film stock.
  • Put a real place-name in the prompt and describe its clutter.
  • Use direct on-camera flash for night portraits.
  • Ask for grain, halation, motion blur, and red-eye.
  • Keep skin unretouched: pores, sweat, stray hairs.
  • Compose off-center and crop tight.
  • Hide hands in pockets or keep them busy.
  • Generate 4–6 variations and pick the least perfect one.
  • Grade all selects together, then add grain last.

Don't

  • “Hyperrealistic,” “8K,” “ultra-detailed,” “masterpiece.”
  • “Perfect skin,” “perfect teeth,” “flawless.”
  • “Cinematic lighting” with no source or direction.
  • Symmetrical, centered, eye-level portraits.
  • HDR, heavy clarity, or over-sharpening.
  • Square 1:1 crops for everything.
  • Upscaling more than once.
  • Recreating real people without rights.
  • Presenting staged AI scenes as documentary.
Words that kill realism Hyperrealistic 8K Ultra-detailed Masterpiece Perfect skin HDR Symmetrical Stock photo Flawless CGI

09 / Use it right

Style is a language,
not a license.

These are original deconstructions of a visual language, not Spike's private recipes and not permission to copy specific frames. Style imitation is normal; passing off someone's identity, community, or lived experience as your own documentary is not.

Don't recreate real people without rights. Don't stage AI favela scenes and present them as journalism. Label AI work where required. If you shoot in a real community, hire locally and pay people. The look is better when the respect is real.
