1. Settling Nondeterministic Communication Complexity of Graph Connectivity

Zhijun Zhang · arXiv:2610.32346 · submitted Oct 23, 2026 · cs.CC

Proves an optimal Ω(N log N) lower bound on the nondeterministic communication complexity of deciding whether an N-vertex graph split between two players is connected, matching the simple spanning-forest protocol; it also gives randomized and bounded-round results.

Why it matters: The abstract calls this one of the longest-standing open problems in communication complexity, open since Babai, Frankl and Simon (FOCS '86); optimal bounds were previously known only for one- and two-round protocols.

2. Proving and Improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs

Randall Balestriero, Yann LeCun · arXiv:2610.39302 · submitted Oct 29, 2026 · cs.AI

Balestriero and LeCun prove a "Platonic Representation Theorem": joint-embedding predictive architectures (JEPAs) trained separately on different modalities learn the same representation up to a rotation whenever their positive pairs are good views of one scene. They argue next-token prediction is a JEPA with bad views.

Why it matters: It turns a widely discussed conjecture about why large models converge into a theorem with stated conditions, and gives a recipe for getting the convergence on purpose.

3. Observation of the T_cc̄1(4430)⁻ state in B⁰ → D*⁻D⁰K⁺ decays

LHCb collaboration, R. Aaij, M. Abdelfatah, A. S. W. Abdelmotteleb et al. (1193 authors) · arXiv:2610.35239 · submitted Oct 26, 2026 · hep-ex

LHCb (1,193 listed authors), using 9 fb⁻¹ of 7, 8 and 13 TeV data, finds known excited D_s⁺ resonances can't describe B⁰ → D*⁻D⁰K⁺ decays; adding the tetraquark T_cc̄1(4430)⁻ fixes the fit at more than 5σ.

Why it matters: A >5σ sighting of an exotic four-quark (tetraquark) state in a new decay channel, a new handle on what such states are made of.

4. Optimal Worst-Case Open Addressing: Braided Cuckoo Hashing

Andrew Krapivin, William Kuszmaul · arXiv:2610.33229 · submitted Oct 23, 2026 · cs.DS

Introduces braided cuckoo hashing, an open-addressed hash table that runs at load factor 1−ε with worst-case O(log 1/ε) queries, insertions and deletions with high probability.

Why it matters: That is an exponential improvement in worst-case update time and disproves a conjecture of Bender, Kuszmaul and Zhou on the query/update trade-off. The paper is listed for SODA 2027.

5. Characteristic gluing to extremal Kerr horizons and the third law of black hole thermodynamics in vacuum

Stefan Czimek · arXiv:2610.35836 · submitted Oct 26, 2026 · math.AP

Czimek proves a non-perturbative characteristic gluing theorem for Einstein's vacuum equations: any Boyer–Lindquist sphere outside a Kerr black hole can be glued along one null hypersurface to the horizon of a sufficiently massive extremal Kerr black hole.

Why it matters: It proves the extremal case of a Kehle–Unger conjecture, bearing on whether the third law of black hole thermodynamics (no reaching extremality) can be violated in vacuum.

6. The Tight Competitive Ratio of Greedy for Online Steiner Forest

Iman Gholami, Ilya Hajiaghayi, MohammadTaghi Hajiaghayi, Peyman Jabbarzade · arXiv:2610.40569 · submitted Oct 29, 2026 · cs.DS

Shows the greedy algorithm for online Steiner Forest is Θ(log k · log log k)-competitive, with a matching lower bound.

Why it matters: It settles greedy's exact ratio and refutes the O(log k) conjecture of Awerbuch, Azar and Bartal (SODA 1996), a 30-year-old question in online network design.

7. Counting Admissible Sets of Integers: A Proof of the Erdős–Nicolas–Sárközy Conjecture

Guoqing Wang, Wenkai Yang · arXiv:2610.34372 · submitted Oct 25, 2026 · math.CO

Proves that the number a(N) of admissible subsets of {1,…,N} (sets where equal-sum subsets have equal size) satisfies log a(N) ≍ √N, with explicit exp(c√N) and exp(C√N) bounds.

Why it matters: Settles a 1991 Erdős–Nicolas–Sárközy counting conjecture in additive combinatorics.

8. Beyond Selected Breakthroughs: Evaluating Language Models on Randomly Sampled Mathematical Conjectures

Helen Jenne, Davis Brown, Jesse He, Henry Kvinge · arXiv:2610.40382 · submitted Oct 29, 2026 · cs.AI

OpenConjecture collects conjectures from arXiv math papers since February 2026 and runs frontier GPT models on 200 randomly sampled ones. The models produced candidate resolutions for about 60%, with counterexamples 1.3× as common as proofs.

Why it matters: It measures AI math on a random sample instead of cherry-picked breakthroughs. The candidates were not checked by experts, so the 60% is an upper bound on real resolutions.

9. SN Ares: A Strongly Lensed Type IIb Supernova at z = 1.276 with a Predicted 70-Year Time Delay

Conor Larison, Justin D. R. Pierel, Matthew Siebert, Tom Resseguier et al. (72 authors) · arXiv:2610.33161 · submitted Oct 23, 2026 · astro-ph.HE

Reports SN Ares, a supernova lensed by the MACS J0308.9+2645 cluster, classified with JWST/NIRSpec spectra as Type IIb at z = 1.276, the highest-redshift spectroscopic member of its class.

Why it matters: Lens models say this is the first image to arrive, with two more due in 70 or more years, an unusually long baseline for time-delay cosmology.

10. Beta decay of potassium-54 reveals the role of two-body currents in neutron-rich nuclei

Zhengyu Xu, Robert Grzywacz, Matthias Heinz, Thomas Papenbrock et al. (87 authors) · arXiv:2610.36218 · submitted Oct 26, 2026 · nucl-ex

A beta-decay measurement of potassium-54 tests ab initio theory far from stability and finds evidence that two-body weak currents explain the quenching of Gamow–Teller strength in neutron-rich nuclei. The arXiv comment says it is accepted in Nature Communications.

Why it matters: Beta decay of neutron-rich nuclei drives heavy-element synthesis; direct tests of two-body currents there had been missing.

11. On Zeros and Algorithms for Disordered Systems II: The Antiferromagnetic Ising Model up to Kesten-Stigum

Ferenc Bencs, Brice Huang, Daniel Z. Lee, Kuikui Liu et al. (5 authors) · arXiv:2610.40598 · submitted Oct 29, 2026 · cs.DS

Gives the first polynomial-time algorithm to estimate the antiferromagnetic Ising partition function on random d-regular graphs at all temperatures up to the Kesten–Stigum threshold, by proving there are no Fisher zeros in a strip around that interval.

Why it matters: This covers the whole replica-symmetric regime, conjectured to be the limit for efficient counting. The authors disclose GPT 5.5 Pro was used for a few minor technical steps and say they verified everything themselves.

12. A counterexample to Tokareva's conjecture on sums of two bent functions

Yansong Feng, Hao Jiang, Guancheng Li, Tianyi Li et al. (5 authors) · arXiv:2610.32239 · submitted Oct 23, 2026 · math.CO

Shows x₁x₂(x₃x₄+x₅x₆+x₇x₈) on 𝔽⁸ is not a sum of two bent functions, disproving Tokareva's conjecture, and extends the counterexample to every even n ≥ 8.

Why it matters: Bent functions matter for cryptographic S-box design. Per the abstract, LLM agents (MiniMax M3.1-Flash-Preview) found the counterexample during the NSUCRYPTO 2026 olympiad.

13. Individually Addressed Logical Pauli Measurements by QLDPC Surgery on a Trapped Ion Quantum Computer

Surabhi Luthra, Alex Owens · arXiv:2610.39067 · submitted Oct 28, 2026 · quant-ph

Runs QLDPC surgery, individually addressed logical Pauli measurements, on a [[10,2,3]] generalized bicycle code on Quantinuum's H2-2 trapped-ion machine, with a 0.72% logical X̄₁ error.

Why it matters: QLDPC codes promise much lower qubit overhead than surface codes, but only if single logical qubits can be operated on inside a block; this is a hardware demonstration of that step.

14. Memory Tax: Dissecting the Net Value of Agent Memory

Xuan He, Sining Zhoubian, Ruozhen Yang, Yuhao Wen et al. (11 authors) · arXiv:2610.34073 · submitted Oct 25, 2026 · cs.CL

A large evaluation finds many dedicated agent-memory systems do worse than naive retrieval-augmented generation over raw history, and splits memory's net value into a "memory benefit" and a "memory tax."

Why it matters: Useful for anyone building long-running agents: the default of adding a memory layer is often a net loss.

15. What Effort Level Should I Choose? On the Economics of Reasoning Effort in LLM Agents

Kaiser Sun, Yongchan Kwon, Zain Hasan, Shang Zhu et al. (6 authors) · arXiv:2610.34491 · submitted Oct 25, 2026 · cs.AI

Treats LLM reasoning-effort settings as a credence good and measures, on proprietary and open models, what higher effort actually buys. Always picking the top level wastes money without guaranteeing the best result; starting low and escalating only after a verified failure matched or beat top effort.

Why it matters: Gives evidence for choosing effort levels on cost, not on the assumption that more is always better.

16. Eat Your Precision: Precision Objective Mitigates Reward Hacking in Rubric-based RL

Hyun Ryu, Jong-Heon Kim, Yiqing Xie, Seungone Kim et al. (6 authors) · arXiv:2610.33512 · submitted Oct 24, 2026 · cs.CL

Proposes the FORK reward, which adds a precision objective to rubric-coverage rewards in RL so trained models stop padding answers with unnecessary or wrong content.

Why it matters: Rubric rewards are common for non-verifiable tasks; this targets a reward-hacking failure that made RL-tuned answers less preferred than the base model's.

17. Trial by Trace: Benchmarking AI Agent Harnesses for Reliability and Failure Attribution

Zhiyong Cao, Hengjie Zhu, Yongxin Wang, Xuchen Li et al. (23 authors) · arXiv:2610.32285 · submitted Oct 23, 2026 · cs.AI

TraceTrial-Bench scores agent harnesses on seven reliability components across 95 expert tasks, 4 harnesses and 9 models (3,420 runs). Harness scores range 59.8–72.0%, and 31.1% of runs succeed at the task while failing a harness criterion.

Why it matters: It separates harness quality from model quality, and shows that task success can hide unreliable agent behavior.

18. Multiscale Policy Optimization for Long-Horizon LLM Agents

Tao Ren, Hui Yang, Jia Deng, Tingyang Chen et al. (19 authors) · arXiv:2610.35013 · submitted Oct 26, 2026 · cs.AI

Multiscale Policy Optimization anchors credit assignment at the turn level, with finer token-level allocation, for training long-horizon LLM agents; it reports consistent gains on search, tool-use and automation tasks with 4B and 30B-A3B models.

Why it matters: 382 upvotes on Hugging Face Daily Papers (Oct 28 list), reflecting interest in better RL for agents.

19. The Trinity: Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents

Weidong Tang, Ruiheng Wang, Jingxuan Zhang, Hexin Wang et al. (20 authors) · arXiv:2610.36077 · submitted Oct 26, 2026 · cs.AI

A recursive self-improvement framework in which Creator, Explorer and Evolver roles generate environments and evolve the agent harness by capability, not by task surface form; reported gains are +34 points across 28 training benchmarks and +26 on 18 unseen ones.

Why it matters: Self-reported but large gains in automated agent improvement; 389 upvotes on Hugging Face Daily Papers (Oct 28 list).
