1. Evidence for the decay $B^0 \to μ^+ μ^-$ and improved measurements of the decay $B^0_s \to μ^+ μ^-$

LHCb Collaboration (1196 authors) · arXiv:2611.00230 · submitted Oct 30, 2026 · hep-ex

Reports the first single-experiment evidence for the rare decay B⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻ and the world's most precise measurements of Bₛ⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻ branching fraction and effective lifetime, using 18 fb⁻¹ of LHCb Upgrade I Run 3 data at 13.6 TeV (2024–2025), combined with earlier runs.

Why it matters: B⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻ is a canonical probe of New Physics in the flavor sector; a single-experiment evidence claim from LHCb Upgrade I, with the world's best Bₛ⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻ precision, is a headline result for Run 3.

2. $K_h$-Minor-Free Graphs Have Balanced Separators of Size $O(h\sqrt{n})$

Jonathan Conroy, Hung Le, Jack Spalding-Jamieson · arXiv:2611.00663 · submitted Oct 31, 2026 · math.CO

Proves that every Kₕ-minor-free graph on n vertices has a balanced separator of size O(h√n), resolving a conjecture of Alon, Seymour and Thomas from 1990. The proof is elementary and uses a short hitting walk on vertex expanders.

Why it matters: The AST90 separator conjecture has been a central open problem in structural graph theory for 36 years; an O(h√n) bound is the natural generalization of the planar Lipton–Tarjan theorem.

3. Limits and Constants of OpenAI's Claimed ETH Refutation

Fabio Dias, Yvens Lima · arXiv:2611.00517 · submitted Oct 31, 2026 · cs.CC

A critical note on OpenAI's claimed deterministic #SAT algorithm that would refute the Exponential Time Hypothesis: it separates the headline tensor-contraction bound from the weaker interpolation algorithm in the body, and clarifies which constants and limits actually follow.

Why it matters: An ETH refutation would reshape fine-grained complexity; this paper documents where the claimed bound is proved versus where a weaker exponent appears, so the community can check the claim.

7. Claw-free perfect graphs are chromatic-choosable

Suchismita Mishra, Narayanan Narayanan, Umesh Shankar · arXiv:2611.01068 · submitted Oct 31, 2026 · math.CO
