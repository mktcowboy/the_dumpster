# Philosophy — PhilArchive Top 20 (scout report, received Oct 18, 2026)

## Stats hunt: the verdict
No public stats source exists for PhilArchive/PhilPapers. Checked: 6 web-search queries (no public API, no most-downloaded/trending page, no per-item public download counts); PhilArchive FAQ and PhilPapers FAQ (downloads are author-facing metrics only); OAI-PMH (metadata only); direct record pages 403 to fetch tool. Evidence the metric exists internally: a 2023–2025 author-run experiment (Roberto Thomas Arruda) reported 41,760 PhilPapers downloads of one e-book — real, just not public. The one public quality signal: front-page "Selected recent additions" feed, recency-ordered and filtered by automatically computed quality indicators including author/work "pro" status (per PhilArchive FAQ).

## Methodology
- Source pool: PhilArchive's "Selected recent additions" feed (https://philarchive.org/recent.html?filter=1), pulled ~98 items via RSS with upload dates, authors, venues, abstracts, direct record URLs.
- Limitation: public recent-feeds cap at 100 items with no pagination → pool covered Oct 12–18 only, not full Sep 18–Oct 18. No public route reaches further back.
- "Top" = ranked by (1) venue prestige (Mind, The Philosophical Review, Philosophical Studies, Synthese, JAPA, Philosophers' Imprint, Philosophy and Phenomenological Research, Ergo, SEP, Oxford Studies in Normative Ethics, Blackwell Companions, AAAI/ACM AIES); (2) author prominence; (3) novelty/timeliness; (4) area diversity. Excluded: dissertations, book reviews, non-English, fringe/self-published, duplicates.
- All titles/authors/dates/venues/URLs verified against PhilArchive's own feed/RSS — nothing invented.

## Top 20 (uploaded Oct 12–18, 2026)
1. [The Limits of Reasonable Disagreement](https://philarchive.org/rec/TAYTLO-7) — Anthony Taylor — Oct 15 — *The Philosophical Review* 135(4), 2026. New defense of political liberalism: reinterprets the "reasonable acceptability" test so liberal principles survive the too-little-overlap objection.
2. [Probability for Trivalent Conditionals](https://philarchive.org/rec/GRPFTW) — Paul Égré, Lorenzo Rossi & Jan Sprenger — Oct 14 — *Mind*, 2026. Unified trivalent theory of conditionals; derives Stalnaker's Thesis in full generality; turns Bayesian updating on conditionals into material-conditional updating.
   - corroboration: listed as in press at *Mind* in the [University of Turin repository](https://iris.unito.it/handle/2318/2142754); companion paper in the Australasian Journal of Philosophy (2025)
   - flag: first circulated Jul 2022 (arXiv)
3. [A Priorism in Moral Epistemology](https://philarchive.org/rec/CHUAPI) — Elijah Chudnoff — Oct 17 — *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, forthcoming. Authoritative SEP survey on whether moral judgments can be justified by reason alone.
   - corroboration: live in the [Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-epistemology-a-priori/)
   - flag: revision of a 2019 entry
4. [Solving the puzzle of forever fitting emotions: justifying and requiring reasons](https://philarchive.org/rec/FANSTP) — Ning Fan — Oct 17 — *Philosophical Studies*, forthcoming. Dissolves the "forever fitting emotions" puzzle by splitting reasons into justifying vs. requiring roles.
5. [The Plurality of Ethical Norms on Belief](https://philarchive.org/rec/QUATPO-6) — Z. Quanbeck — Oct 14 — *Oxford Studies in Normative Ethics*, forthcoming. Against Kantian respect-monism in the ethics of belief; norms on belief come from many sources with different strengths.
6. [Agent-Relative Prerogatives to Harm Enemies](https://philarchive.org/rec/LANAPT-5) — Benjamin Lange — Oct 14 — *Oxford Studies in Normative Ethics* vol. 17, forthcoming. Defends permissions to harm enemies in protective contexts grounded in "negative partiality."
7. [Love in Personal and Political Life](https://philarchive.org/rec/PRELIP-2) — Ryan Preston-Roedder — Oct 15 — *Philosophers' Imprint*, forthcoming. Black American philosophical ideal of answering racial injustice with love, via James Baldwin's early fiction and essays.
8. [Dark Comedy: Immorality, Perspectives and Amusement](https://philarchive.org/rec/WALDCI-6) — Zoe Walker — Oct 14 — *Ergo*, forthcoming. What makes a joke immoral (perspective invited, sustaining injustice) — and why that has no bearing on whether it's funny.
9. [Self-Knowledge through Reflection](https://philarchive.org/rec/BOYSTR) — Matthew Boyle — Oct 15 — forthcoming *Oxford Handbook of Self-Knowledge*. Rationalist defense: mere reflection yields immediate self-knowledge.
10. [The Boulder Problem](https://philarchive.org/rec/SRITBP) — Vishnu Sridharan & Thomas Byrne — Oct 16 — *Philosophical Perspectives*, forthcoming. Live disagreement: does the ban on killing one to save five extend to letting yourself kill one to save five?
11. [Real Consciousness or Real Illusion? A Higher-Order Approach to AI Consciousness](https://philarchive.org/rec/BRORCO-5) — Richard Brown — Oct 14 — manuscript. Higher-order theory meets AI: computational functionalists should take LLM consciousness seriously; biological naturalists should be illusionists.
12. [Can Emotions Be Rational?](https://philarchive.org/rec/MLLCEB) — Jean Moritz Müller & Hichem Naar — Oct 16 — *Philosophy Compass* 21(5), 2026. Survey case for substantive emotional rationality — emotions as genuine responses to reasons.
13. [Conspiracy Theories and Belief in Conspiracy Theories](https://philarchive.org/rec/HARCTA-21) — Keith Harris — Oct 15 — *Philosophy Compass*, 2026. State-of-the-art overview of what conspiracy theories are and why people believe them.
14. [Self-application and Higher-orderese](https://philarchive.org/rec/NAVSAH) — William Nava — Oct 17 — *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research*, forthcoming. Defends self-applicative predication; metaphysics should use a higher-order language allowing it.
15. [An Algebraic Approach to Necessities](https://philarchive.org/rec/CLEAAA-6) — Antonio Maria Cleani — Oct 12 — manuscript. Reworks Bacon's higher-order account of necessity via algebraic semantics; lattice of necessities is a Heyting algebra.
16. [Diverging Paths to Dark-Matter Discovery](https://philarchive.org/rec/ALLDPT) — Simon Allzén — Oct 17 — manuscript. 201,627 ADS records show how dark-matter research reorganized after the WIMP lost its central position.
17. [The Perils and Prospects of NeuroAI](https://philarchive.org/rec/BARTPA-39) — David Barack — Oct 15 — forthcoming Routledge volume. Splits NeuroAI into Strong (quietist, premature) vs. Weak (hypothesis-generating testbed already bearing fruit).
18. [AI Alignment and Fiduciary Obligation](https://philarchive.org/rec/LANAAA-12) — Benjamin Lange — Oct 12 — *Proc. AAAI/ACM AIES* 9(3), 2026. Grounds AI alignment in fiduciary duties — loyalty, care, good faith, candour — developers owe users.
19. [Beyond ruler and ruled: legitimacy, power differences, and plebeian responses to resurgent oligarchy](https://philarchive.org/rec/PRIBRA-7) — Janosch Prinz & Andy Scerri — Oct 15 — *Political Studies*, forthcoming. Realist legitimacy theory: tripartite ruler / oligarchic-ruled / ordinary-ruled model.
20. [Do Christians and Muslims Worship the Same God? A Philosophical Investigation](https://philarchive.org/rec/SIJDCA) — Joshua Sijuwade — Oct 17 — *Perichoresis* 24(3), 2026. Trinitarian and Tawhidic conceptions ascribe logically contradictory essential properties — different divine entities.

Strong alternates cut for space: Bickerstaff on Aristotle vs. the Heracliteans (*Metaphysica*), Fillmore-Patrick on inductive logic (*Synthese*), Green on Molyneux's question (*Locke Studies*), Leuenberger on authenticity and meaning (JAPA), Maung on suicide and mental illness (Oxford Handbook), Heersmink & Michaelian on 4E memory and technology, Rudolph on Frankfurt and generative AI (*Critical AI*).
