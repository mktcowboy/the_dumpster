Why the Dow is different

The Dow is price-weighted: a $500 stock carries ten times the weight of a $50 stock, regardless of market value. A 2-for-1 split therefore cuts a stock’s weight roughly in half unless the divisor is adjusted.

Index weights drift every day with prices. Membership changes reset them; the divisor keeps the index level continuous.

Data sources, coverage & method

Membership: Historical components of the Dow Jones Industrial Average (Wikipedia, CC BY-SA), retrieved October 21, 2026. The roster in force at each month-end is used. Prices: Yahoo Finance, monthly unadjusted closes converted back from split- and spinoff-adjusted history; latest weights use the October 20, 2026 close.

Weights are price ÷ sum of the 30 prices. Because the Dow is price-weighted, this is also the index’s portfolio weight. Monthly weights use the last trading day of each month; the report begins in April 2004, when Pfizer, AIG, and Verizon replaced AT&T, Kodak, and International Paper.

Coverage. DuPont is excluded from historical weights: Yahoo’s pre-2019 DD series does not reconcile with DuPont’s reported quarterly price ranges after the DowDuPont merger and 2019 split. General Motors (2004–2009), Kraft Foods (2008–2012), DowDuPont (2017–2019), and Walgreens Boots Alliance (2018–2024) are delisted and have no accessible unadjusted history. Weights are renormalized over the priced members; the number priced is shown in the chart tooltip and era cards. The current roster is fully priced.

Checks. The method reproduces the published June 29, 2026 divisor of 0.16824816528350 and the December 31, 2019 price sum of 4,207.87 once Walgreens is added back. At March 31, 2008, the priced sum reconciles with the published divisor after allowing for the excluded DuPont and GM prices. Current weights match a published Dow weights snapshot within normal one-day price drift (this report uses the prior close).

Splits and spinoffs are handled by multiplying a month-end close by every split ratio dated after that month-end, recovering the price actually quoted at the time. This matters for names such as Apple, Nvidia, Nike, Walmart, GE, AIG, Citigroup, AT&T, Honeywell, and 3M.

Not investment advice. Prices and memberships can contain errors; the original sources are linked above.
