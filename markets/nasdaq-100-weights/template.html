Not a price-weighted index

The Nasdaq-100 is modified market-cap weighted: a company’s share of the index tracks its market value, then rebalance caps and adjustments keep the largest weights within the index’s limits. A high share price alone does not raise a member’s weight.

Methodology, coverage & sources

Construction. The Nasdaq-100 is a modified market-capitalization-weighted index. Base weights follow market value; at rebalances, caps and adjustments limit the largest constituents. It is not price-weighted: a stock’s quoted price does not determine its index share.

Three views. The method switch updates every weight-based section below. Official capped uses the index’s adjusted weights, uncapped shows raw market-cap shares, and QQQ uses the fund’s published holdings weights. The membership log is identical for all three.

Definitions. Top-10 share sums the ten largest weights. Effective N is 1 ÷ Σw². Averages ignore months with no reported weight. The stacked “Others” band is the residual to 100%, so it also absorbs any unpriced members in a partial-coverage month.
